# Lullaby lyrics and cultures: themes, structure and Indian traditions (for writing ORIGINAL Cheeko lullabies)

Scope note for the report writer: these notes cover lyric content and structure, plus Indian language traditions. They describe patterns only. No song lyrics are reproduced; refrain words are named only where needed (2-3 words at most). Research done 2026-10-09. Sahapedia was searched for but no Sahapedia page appeared in results (see Gaps under the Indian traditions question).

---

## 1. What are lullabies about worldwide, and which older dark themes do modern writers avoid?

### Takeaway
Across cultures the stable core is sleep and night-time calm, the mother (far more than the father), praise and blessing of the child, nature, religion or protection, and hopes for the child's future. Traditional lullabies also often carry lament, threat ("sleep or the ogre/crab will get you"), death and the singer's own worries. Western lullabies became gentler only in the 19th century. A modern children's product should keep the core and drop the threat, death and lament layer.

### Cited Findings
**How universal lullabies are**
- Aubinet (University of Oslo) coded all 186 societies in the Standard Cross-Cultural Sample. Under a strict definition (a conserved repertoire of infant-soothing songs), lullabies were not even a "near universal". Under a broad definition (any singing used to soothe children), 96.8% of societies had them — [HRAF summary of Aubinet, "Lullabies and Universality: An Ethnographic Review", Cross-Cultural Research 2024](https://hraf.yale.edu/ehc/documents/1528)
- A press summary of the same researcher's work reports a 124-culture analysis. Song was used to help children sleep in 97% of them, but only 78% had a specific repertoire called lullabies. Others used popular music, religious songs, improvisation and humming — [Science Norway / University of Oslo](https://partner.sciencenorway.no/history-music-sleep/lullabies-are-not-as-innocent-as-people-think/2507336). (The 186 vs 124 sample sizes differ between the two summaries. Probably different subsets or analyses, not confirmed.)
- Listeners can identify lullabies from unfamiliar cultures. In Mehr et al. (Current Biology, 2018), about 750 people in 60 countries heard 14-second excerpts. Lullabies and dance songs were the most confidently identified functions. Lullabies were slower and melodically simpler than dance songs — [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2018/01/music-may-transcend-cultural-boundaries-to-become-universally-human); [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/your-brain-knows-what-songs-are-no-matter-where-they-came-180967946/)
- In a rating study with 500 listeners in India and 500 in the US, lullabies scored lower than other song types on melodic complexity and rhythmic complexity. They also had a slower tempo and a less steady beat — [Harvard Gazette (2018)](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/music-may-transcend-cultural-boundaries-to-become-universally-human/)

**Common themes**
- A content analysis of lullabies from EU countries found these themes: sleep, religion, mother, baby talk and ethical messages. The mother appears more often than the father — [University of Ioannina repository](https://olympias2.lib.uoi.gr/items/69c614d2-613c-4474-a4b4-5b32ca2de02b)
- A comparison of English, Persian and Azari lullabies found shared motifs of absent fathers, "maternal whispers" and religion — [Masaryk University publication record](https://www.ics.muni.cz/en/research/publications/2227323)
- Many lullabies resemble prayers, and it is often unclear whether they address the child, God or a protecting saint. Most lullabies in Belgium and Northern and Western Europe are cheerful and hopeful — [Science Norway](https://partner.sciencenorway.no/history-music-sleep/lullabies-are-not-as-innocent-as-people-think/2507336)
- Studies of Brahui and Iranian lullabies describe mothers who praise the child, pray for long life and success, and also voice their own grief. Mothers "comfort their own souls" while soothing the child — [Brahui lullaby study, 2017 (tehqeeqat.org)](https://tehqeeqat.org/downloadpdf/29753); [Iranian lullaby study (cfl.modares.ac.ir)](https://cfl.modares.ac.ir/article-11-69314-en.pdf) (both via search-result summaries only)
- Indian lullabies across states commonly feature the moon watching over the baby, animals (sparrows, cows, parrots, crickets), values (kindness, honesty), village scenes and family bonds — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/parenting/indian-lullabies-sleep-babies-learning-culture-language-benefits-parenting-10879782) (popular source)

**Dark and threatening themes in traditional lullabies**
- Aubinet reports "a vast variation", from cheerful songs to ones with "morbid and violent lyrics". Examples include a Slovenian lament by an unmarried mother to an unwanted child, Russian and Baltic lullabies describing a child's funeral, Japanese nannies' songs voicing frustration, and Rock-a-bye Baby's falling child. He links sleep and death (Hypnos and Thanatos were twins) and notes high historical child mortality — [Science Norway](https://partner.sciencenorway.no/history-music-sleep/lullabies-are-not-as-innocent-as-people-think/2507336)
- In 19th-century Western Europe, lullabies became gentler under a Romantic view of children as innocent — [Science Norway](https://partner.sciencenorway.no/history-music-sleep/lullabies-are-not-as-innocent-as-people-think/2507336)
- Rock-a-bye Baby first appears in print in 1765 (Mother Goose's Melody). A 1791 printing attached a moral warning to "the proud and ambitious". The common reading of the ending is a baby falling from a treetop. Political readings (James II's heir) are speculative — [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/behind-the-history-and-the-meaning-of-the-nursery-rhyme-rock-a-bye-baby/?nb=1)
- Threat lullabies elsewhere include Haiti's Dodo Titit (a crab will eat a child who stays awake), France's Le Grand Lustucru (an ogre eats children who don't sleep) and Iceland's Bíum Bíum Bambaló (a figure lurking outside) — [HuffPost list](https://www.huffingtonpost.ca/entry/18-dark-and-disturbing-lullabies-from-around-the-world_us_5af9eacee4b044dfffb4faab) (listicle, weak source)
- A peer-reviewed study of Lithuanian lullabies concluded that intimidating lullabies served "scaring and didactics" — [Vilnius University journal](https://journals.vu.lt/td/article/view/32742)
- Lorca noted the "depth of sadness" in Spanish lullabies and argued they let mothers voice worries. The Gaelic "Ba, Ba, Mo Leanabh Beag" (1848) reflects the potato famine. Vietnamese lullabies are often melancholy — [Wikipedia: Lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lullaby)
- Indian example: a mid-18th-century western Bengal lullaby reflected fear of Maratha raiders (Bargis). When it travelled to Chittagong, the word changed to tidal surges — [Daily Star / Asia News Network, 2 Oct 2026](https://asianews.network/?p=305355)
- Some Indian folk song traditions next to lullabies (e.g., Bhojpuri songs) carry the anguish women suffer at the hands of the mother-in-law as a recurring theme — Haris Upadhyaya's Bhojpuri folksong collection as described in [Asian Ethnology articles (search summary; exact article unconfirmed)](https://asianethnology.org/article/1318/download); [also](https://asianethnology.org/article/1811/download)

### Inferences
- Safe core themes for Cheeko: sleep and rest, moon and stars, night animals settling down, the parent's love and nearness, nature winding down (birds to nests, flowers closing, river slowing), gentle blessings and hopes ("may you grow strong and kind"), and tomorrow's small joys.
- Themes to avoid even though they are traditional: threats or bogeymen ("sleep or X will come"), falling or injury, death or funerals, a parent leaving or being absent, the singer's grief or complaints, raiders or disasters, and in-law conflict. Also avoid moralising "warnings". Lithuanian research shows these were used to scare and discipline.
- The "fear of night and the unknown" motif can be flipped: the night is friendly, the moon is watching, everyone is safe and home.

### Gaps
- No quantitative cross-cultural frequency table of lullaby themes was found (e.g., % of lullabies mentioning the moon). The Greek EU study's full text is mostly in Greek and could not be read.
- Brahui and Iranian study findings come from search-result summaries, not full texts.

## 2. Structure: refrains, line length, syllables, rhyme, soothing syllables, number of verses, fade-out, the child's name

### Takeaway
Lullabies are short, repetitive, slow, mostly in a rocking triple or 6/8 feel, and are carried by a refrain of soothing vocables (aaraaro, laali, jo jo, ninni, lori, hush). The research stresses repetition, simplicity, stretched vowels, falling melodies and pauses. Many traditions use "rocking words" with little or no meaning. Length is elastic: traditional singers extend or shorten a lullaby to match the child's drowsiness.

### Cited Findings
**Musical shape that lyrics must fit**
- Typical lullaby traits include simple pitch contours, mostly consonant intervals, tonic-dominant harmony, repetition, long pauses between sections, usually triple meter or 6/8 ("swinging or rocking motion"), slow tempo, short utterances, and almost never instrumental accompaniment — [Wikipedia: Lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lullaby)
- Infants respond more to unaccompanied than to accompanied singing (Ilari & Sundara, 2009). 2- and 4-month-olds prefer consonant over dissonant intervals (Trainor, Tsang & Cheung, 2002) — cited in [Wikipedia: Lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lullaby)
- Infant-directed singing is higher in pitch and slower in tempo than singing to adults. Listeners across cultures can tell which version was for the infant, and infants prefer the infant-directed version — [Trehub et al. 1993 (Trainor Lab PDF)](https://trainorlab.mcmaster.ca/publications/pdfs/1993_Trehub.pdf); [Trainor 1996](https://trainorlab.mcmaster.ca/publications/pdfs/trainor_1996.pdf)
- Repeating rhythms and contours, plus stretched-out vowels, are described in lullabies from Vietnam, Norway, Colombia, Afghanistan and Indigenous communities in Panama and North America. Simplicity and repetitiveness were the cues listeners used to identify lullabies. Falling melodic contours may be another shared trait — [Trehub et al. 1993](https://trainorlab.mcmaster.ca/publications/pdfs/1993_Trehub.pdf)
- Adults still identified lullabies when the words were filtered out, so the sung performance carries much of the "lullaby" signal — [Trehub et al. 1993](https://trainorlab.mcmaster.ca/publications/pdfs/1993_Trehub.pdf)
- Laurel Trainor describes lullabies as "very smooth and legato" without strong accents — [podcast transcript, Ep. 137](https://mpetersonmusic.com/ep-137-transcript)
- Infants relaxed (lower heart rate, smaller pupils, reduced electrodermal activity) to lullabies in unfamiliar foreign languages more than to other song types. Bainbridge: "Melody is one of the things that sticks out for lullabies." — [Harvard Gazette on Bainbridge, Bertolo, Mehr et al., Nature Human Behaviour 2020](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2020/10/research-shows-lullabies-in-any-language-relax-babies)

**Soothing syllables, vocables and "rocking words"**
- Hungarian cradle songs often use meaningless, archaic or baby-talk "rocking words". Socotra lullabies repeat nonsense syllables. One Dutch lullaby's text was "mostly chosen for its rhyme". Many lullabies are hypnotic regardless of their words — [Wikipedia: Lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lullaby)
- Punjabi loris: some carry meaningful verse, while "others have no special meanings yet have a very sweet sound" that helps babies sleep — [Oriental College Magazine, Punjab University, Lahore](https://ocm.pu.edu.pk/website/journal/article/646312e5bdc4b/abstract)
- Tamil thalattu are "characterized by repetitive refrains" such as aararo aariraro — [Sasikumar & Thamburaj, NIE/NTU Singapore, ICOLLITE abstract](https://icollite.upi.edu/file/ppt/ABS-_ICOLLITE-25184-_The_Evolution_and_Socio-Cultural_Reflections_of_Tamil_Lullabies.pdf). The name thalattu is linked to "thal" (tongue): tongue movement makes a melodious sound at the start — [Wikipedia: Lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lullaby)
- Regional refrain syllables listed in one roundup: "laali" (Telugu), "jo jo" (Kannada), aararo (Tamil), "ninni ninni" and "aa ja re" as sound patterns, "lalla ... lori" and "nindiya" (Hindi), "aaye ghum" (Bengali, sleep come) — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/parenting/indian-lullabies-sleep-babies-learning-culture-language-benefits-parenting-10879782)
- A family lullaby built on "ninni" shows heavy oral variation, with several versions and no settled translation — [Mama Lisa's World](https://www.mamalisa.com/blog/can-anyone-help-with-a-lullaby-from-india-called-ninni-buba-ninni)
- Bengali rhymes are slow and repetitive, "mirroring a resting pulse". They share features of infant-directed speech: stretched vowels, higher pitch and predictable repetition — [Daily Star / ANN](https://asianews.network/?p=305355)

**Length, verses and fade-out**
- The length of a Tamil thalattu "can vary based on the mother's emotional state and the child's sleep", so the singer stretches or cuts it — [ICOLLITE abstract](https://icollite.upi.edu/file/ppt/ABS-_ICOLLITE-25184-_The_Evolution_and_Socio-Cultural_Reflections_of_Tamil_Lullabies.pdf)
- NICU singing guidance: if the infant shows stress, reduce complexity by humming the song instead of singing the words. The protocol introduces sound first (live lullaby), then touch, then rocking — [Dräger NICU music document](https://www.draeger.com/Content/Documents/Content/NICU_Music_Final.pdf)
- A choral performance note says to land the last held syllable on an open "Ah" vowel to keep pitch steady — [sjmp.com preview](https://sjmp.com/catalog/music/motcombe-angels-carol/preview) (choral context, not lullaby-specific)

**Rhyme, syllables and the child's name**
- A classroom lullaby-writing handout lists rhyme, the same number of syllables per line, and parallel structure (same parts of speech in the same order) as core tools. It names the moon as a common lullaby symbol (light and comfort in darkness) — [Local Learning Network, "Write our Own Lullaby" handout](https://locallearningnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Lally-Write-our-Own-Lullaby.pdf)
- Name placement advice: put the name once in a refrain line that stands on its own, then add 2-3 short lines about being safe, loved and ready for sleep. A short name fits inside one musical phrase, while a longer name may need its own phrase. Read lines aloud at a calm pace so the name never sounds rushed — [macmyths guide](https://macmyths.com/how-to-make-a-personalized-lullaby-with-your-childs-name/) (blog)
- "Even just singing their name, maybe with an added rhyming word" is a special musical moment. Children love repetition, and it helps them developmentally — [Big Heart World](https://www.bigheartworld.org/?p=11882)
- Carnegie Hall's Lullaby Project custom songs often contain the child's name. Program leaders describe them as "a little bespoke piece" — [The 74 Million](https://www.the74million.org/zero2eight/carnegie-halls-lullaby-project-helps-parents-write-love-songs-to-their-infants/). Indian parenting advice also suggests adding the child's name to lullaby lyrics — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/parenting/indian-lullabies-sleep-babies-learning-culture-language-benefits-parenting-10879782)
- The famous Malayalam lullaby Omanathinkal Kidavo (1813) never uses the word for "sleep". It soothes through a long chain of "is it this, or that?" comparisons of the child to the moon, flowers, birds, gems and pearls, and through its raga — [Wikipedia: Omanathinkal Kidavo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omanathinkal_Kidavo)

### Inferences
A practical structure for Cheeko lullabies (derived from the above, not a published standard):
- **Form:** refrain, verse, refrain, verse, refrain, then a hummed or vocable-only tail. Two or three short verses suit a 2-4 minute track. The refrain carries the soothing vocable plus, optionally, the child's name.
- **Lines:** short and even. About 5-8 syllables per line for ages 0-5, up to 8-10 for 6-8. Keep the syllable count matched line-to-line within a stanza so the tune repeats exactly.
- **Rhyme:** simple AABB or ABCB couplets. End rhymes on open vowels or soft voiced endings (-aa, -o, -ee, -n, -ng, -m, -l) rather than stops (-t, -k, -p). In Hindi this favours endings such as -aa, -i, -o, -re, -na, -ri.
- **Fade-out:** in the last refrain, drop words step by step (full line, then name plus vocable, then vocable only, then hum). Lengthen the final vowel and add a long pause. This copies the NICU "move to humming" advice and the falling-contour, long-pause traits.
- **Elastic length:** because traditional lullabies stretch with the child's drowsiness, the device could loop the refrain or the hum section, or offer a "sleepy tail", rather than hard-stopping.
- **The word "sleep" is optional.** Imagery-led lullabies (moon, river, birds going home) can soothe without commanding "sleep" over and over. The Omanathinkal example shows this works.
- **Name slot:** one refrain line with a name slot that fits names of 1-4 syllables. Keep the name on a held note, never in a fast run. Plan a name-free fallback (e.g., a pet name such as "little one", "chhotu", "munna/munni" or "baby").

### Gaps
- No empirical source was found giving typical syllable counts, verse counts or rhyme-scheme frequencies for lullabies. The numbers above are craft inferences.
- No study was found that measures how traditional lullabies end (fade, ritardando, humming) beyond the NICU guidance and the general "long pauses, falling contour" findings.
- The writing.codidact.com and qz.com songwriting pages returned HTTP 403, so their full advice could not be checked beyond search snippets.

## 3. Vocabulary and language for different ages (0-2, 3-5, 6-8)

### Takeaway
For babies and toddlers, sound matters more than meaning: repetition, vocables, the child's name and soft vowels. For preschoolers, rhyme and predictable line endings support phonological awareness, so rhyming refrains and "finish the line" slots are useful. For 6-8 year olds no lullaby-specific research was found. A short, calm narrative with richer imagery is a reasonable inference.

### Cited Findings
- Infants prefer infant-directed singing (higher pitch, slower tempo, more emotional voice) — [Trainor 1996](https://trainorlab.mcmaster.ca/publications/pdfs/trainor_1996.pdf); [Trehub et al. 1993](https://trainorlab.mcmaster.ca/publications/pdfs/1993_Trehub.pdf)
- Mothers reproduce their songs with remarkable consistency across sessions: about 3.7 bpm and 1.1 semitone mean differences, versus about 23 bpm and 5 semitones for repeated speech. Repetition and sameness are part of the genre — [search summary of Bergeson & Trehub (Trainor Lab publications)](https://trainorlab.mcmaster.ca/publications/TrehubEtAl1997)
- Some Punjabi loris are valued mainly for "sweet sound" rather than meaning — [Punjab University, Lahore](https://ocm.pu.edu.pk/website/journal/article/646312e5bdc4b/abstract). Hungarian cradle songs use meaningless "rocking words" and baby talk — [Wikipedia: Lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lullaby)
- The Center for Early Literacy Learning reviewed 12 studies of 5,299 preschoolers. Nursery-rhyme recitation, rhyme experiences, and the ability to supply the last word of familiar rhymes were related to rhyme production, rhyme detection and phoneme awareness (Dunst et al., 2011). In Harper (2011), pre-K children given explicit nursery-rhyme instruction outperformed controls on rhyme awareness and completion — [search summary of CELL review and Harper 2011, ERIC](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1097164.pdf) (secondary summary)
- Caregiver singing in infancy is associated with later vocabulary size (Franco et al., 2021). This is correlational. Rhymes at phrase endings in a predictable rhythm may give infants a perceptual advantage — [Frontiers in Psychology 2021 brief report](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.680882/pdf); [PMC8450347](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8450347)
- Writing guides recommend images from the child's own world (moonlit room, favourite blanket, a stuffed animal, favourite food, colour or animal) and the day's events, toys, or "ears, nose and toes" — [macmyths](https://macmyths.com/how-to-make-a-personalized-lullaby-with-your-childs-name/); [Local Learning Network handout](https://locallearningnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Lally-Write-our-Own-Lullaby.pdf); [Big Heart World](https://www.bigheartworld.org/?p=11882)
- The amount and quality of language children hear between 0 and 5 affects language and brain development (stated in a writing guide, not a primary study) — [Big Heart World](https://www.bigheartworld.org/?p=11882)
- Bengali psychologist Dr Md Azharul Islam describes lullabies as "co-regulation" (caregiver and child physiology aligning). He contrasts this with fast-paced screen content that can overload young brains at bedtime — [Daily Star / ANN](https://asianews.network/?p=305355)

### Inferences
- **Ages 0-2 (sound-first):** a vocabulary of about 30-60 very common concrete words: moon, star, sleep, mama, papa, baby, bird, cow, milk, night, soft, warm. Heavy vocable use (aaraaro, laali, jo jo, ninni, lori, shh, hmm, aa-aa). The name repeated. One image per stanza. Onomatopoeia should be soft (hoo-hoo owl, shh-shh wind, tip-tip rain), never sharp (bang, crash).
- **Ages 3-5 (rhyme and routine):** predictable end-rhymes and a refrain the child can join. Use counting-down or "everyone is going to sleep" lists (bird in nest, calf by cow, fish in pond, moon in sky). Leave a "finish the line" gap only in early verses, since it is stimulating, and not in the closing refrain. Gentle sensory words: soft, warm, quiet, slow, sleepy.
- **Ages 6-8 (gentle story):** a calm narrative arc (a small journey that ends at home), richer imagery and a little more vocabulary. Include a reflective "tomorrow" or gratitude line. Keep tempo and repetition just as slow. Avoid suspense, cliff-hangers or quiz-like content at bedtime.
- For all ages, the line about everyone and everything being safe and settled should come last.

### Gaps
- No research was found on lullaby (as opposed to nursery-rhyme) lyrics for children aged 6-8, or on whether older children benefit from lullabies specifically.
- No Hindi- or Indian-language-specific research was found on age-graded vocabulary in children's songs.

## 4. Indian lullaby traditions by language: themes, refrains, cultural references, folk vs film

### Takeaway
Every major Indian language has a named lullaby genre with a signature vocable refrain. Shared motifs are the moon as a kin figure (Chanda mama / Chad mama), personified Sleep (nindiya, Bengali "sleep aunts"), the cradle (jhoola, palna, thottil), praise of the child through comparisons to the moon, gems and flowers, prosperity wishes, family (especially the maternal uncle, mama), local nature, and in some traditions deities (Krishna, Venkateswara). Many lullabies Indians think of as "traditional" are actually Hindi film songs (1940s to 1970s) and remain copyrighted.

### Cited Findings
**Names of genres and refrains**
- Hindi and many Indian languages use *lori*. Malayalam has *tharattu pattu*, Tamil *thalattu*, Telugu *jola* / *jola pata*, Marathi *angai geet*, Odia *nanabaya gita* and Bengali *ghum-parani gaan* — [Wikipedia: Lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lullaby)
- Further terms: Bengali *ghum-er gaan*, Kashmiri *laan-xun*, Marathi *palna geet* (cradle song) and *angai geet*, Kannada *laali* songs. Refrains: Telugu "laali", Kannada "jo jo", Tamil aararo — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/parenting/indian-lullabies-sleep-babies-learning-culture-language-benefits-parenting-10879782)
- Kannada *jōguḷa* = a lullaby or cradle song, or any lulling song — [Alar Kannada-English dictionary](https://www.alar.ink/dictionary/kannada/english/ಜೋಗುಳ); [Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/jogula)
- Tamil has many lullaby names: thalattu, rarattu, thalaelo, tharatu, orattu, olattu, rorattu, thottilpattu (cradle song) and thiruthalatu — [International Research Journal of Tamil, 2022](https://irjt.iorpress.org/index.php/irjt/article/view/1375)
- Marathi *palane* are songs Maharashtrian women sing to put toddlers to sleep — [Saregama (search summary)](https://www.saregama.com/regional/marathi_13/old/song)

**Tamil thalattu**
- Mentioned in Tolkappiyam and Sangam texts (Paripaadal, Natrinai). Periyazhwar (Bhakti period) is credited with the first composed thalattu. Sung by mothers and grandmothers. Themes: maternal affection, rural daily life, hopes, anxieties, devotion. Form: repetitive refrains (aararo aariraro) and simple melodies. Some are linked to Carnatic ragas such as Nilambari. Declining among younger mothers. Revived through recordings (e.g., the album Vatsalyam), stage shows and online initiatives in the COVID era — [Sasikumar & Thamburaj, NIE/NTU Singapore](https://icollite.upi.edu/file/ppt/ABS-_ICOLLITE-25184-_The_Evolution_and_Socio-Cultural_Reflections_of_Tamil_Lullabies.pdf)
- Lyric content includes the mother's hardship before childbirth, prayers for a child, questioning why the child cries, and elaborate praise of the child. Prosperity words recur (wealth, pearls, corals, cattle, buffaloes). The child is compared to seasonal mangoes, and the father and family are praised — [same source](https://icollite.upi.edu/file/ppt/ABS-_ICOLLITE-25184-_The_Evolution_and_Socio-Cultural_Reflections_of_Tamil_Lullabies.pdf)
- Tamil thalattu also cover the child's features, household scenes and cradle movement — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/parenting/indian-lullabies-sleep-babies-learning-culture-language-benefits-parenting-10879782)

**Malayalam tharattu**
- *Omanathinkal Kidavo* was composed in 1813 by Irayimman Thampi, commissioned by Maharani Gowri Lakshmi Bayi for the infant Swathi Thirunal. It was originally in raga Kurinji and Adi tala, and is now often sung in Navaroj or Nilambari. It praises the child through comparisons to nature, precious objects and religious symbols, often as "is it this, or that?". It never uses the word for sleep — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omanathinkal_Kidavo). Thampi was the prince's maternal uncle and lived 1782-1856. Sources disagree on his birth year (1782 vs 1783) — [Wikipedia: Irayimman Thampi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irayimman_Thampi); [swathithirunal.in](https://www.swathithirunal.in/?p=5594)
- In 2013 the Irayimman Thampi Memorial Trust alleged that the opening of the Oscar-nominated "Pi's Lullaby" was a Tamil translation of this lullaby. Bombay Jayashri denied it, and no ruling was found — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omanathinkal_Kidavo); [Business Standard (G Sreekumar)](https://www.business-standard.com/article/beyond-business/a-case-of-two-lullabies-113031500573_1.html)
- Malayalam tharattu commonly evoke rivers, coconut trees and green Kerala landscapes — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/parenting/indian-lullabies-sleep-babies-learning-culture-language-benefits-parenting-10879782)

**Telugu jola / laali paata**
- Annamacharya (15th century) is credited with the lullaby *Jo Achyutananda*. The temple's daily cycle at Tirumala includes a wake-up song and a lullaby (jola pata) for the deity's bedtime rite (Ekanta seva) — [Wikipedia: Annamacharya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annamacharya); [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/culture/do-you-know-who-set-to-tune-the-immortal-song-kurai-ondrum-illai-most-likely-you-wouldnt) (notes a well-known setting in raga Kaapi rather than Nilambari)
- Telugu "laali" songs focus on the child's smile and presence — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/parenting/indian-lullabies-sleep-babies-learning-culture-language-benefits-parenting-10879782)

**Kannada jogula**
- Kannada cradle songs describe the baby, the rocking motion and the stillness of night. Laali songs reflect home, togetherness and parental hopes — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/parenting/indian-lullabies-sleep-babies-learning-culture-language-benefits-parenting-10879782)

**Bengali ghum parani gaan / chhora**
- Mothers address *Ghumparani masi-pisi*, the maternal and paternal "aunts of sleep", asking them to bring sleep. *Chad mama* (Uncle Moon) is asked to place a blessing mark (tip) on the child's forehead. The *chader buri*, an old woman on the moon, spins thread. Bengali chhora and cradle songs are dated to at least a thousand years. Tagore collected about 100 in the late 19th century. Yogindranath Sarkar's *Khukumonir Chhara* (1899) was the first printed compendium. Bangla Academy researchers documented about 5,000 rhymes in the 1960s — [Adrin Sarwar, The Daily Star via ANN, 2 Oct 2026](https://asianews.network/?p=305355)
- Composed (authored) Bengali lullaby-themed songs exist alongside the folk tradition and should not be treated as folk — [Sarkarverse wiki (search summary)](https://sarkarverse.org/wiki/Rater_belay_sabai_ghumay)

**Gujarati halardu / halarda**
- A Central University of Gujarat study (researcher Devraj Raol, guide Dr Minalba Jadeja) sampled 14 of 120 halarda. Moral values (love, affection, joy, fearlessness, generosity, security, humanity) appeared in 8 of 14, and social values (women's status, brotherhood, harmony) in 6. Personal values (food, health, duty, entrepreneurship, self-efficacy) and spiritual values (devotion to God) appeared in 4 each, and national values (environment, patriotism, freedom) in 2 — [CUG abstract PDF](https://www.cugujarat.ac.in/AllDocuments/Department/ResearchProgramme/Abstract%20Data/Education/Dr.%20Minalba%20Jadeja/M.A/8_M.A._Devraj.pdf)
- A Rajkot folk lullaby recording is described as "recited in a prolonged melody with no accompaniment" — [Koizumi archive, Tokyo University of the Arts (search summary)](https://koizumi.geidai.ac.jp/?p=133787)
- Gujarati cradle songs feature the child's innocence and gentle home moments. Rajasthani loris feature desert winds, camels and slow village nights — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/parenting/indian-lullabies-sleep-babies-learning-culture-language-benefits-parenting-10879782)

**Punjabi lori**
- The lori is a very famous folk song for babies in Punjab. Some have meaningful verse and some are valued for sound alone. Loris reflect Punjabi culture and tastes — [Oriental College Magazine, Punjab University, Lahore](https://ocm.pu.edu.pk/website/journal/article/646312e5bdc4b/abstract) (Pakistani Punjab focus)
- Punjabi loris often describe the moon, fairies and the warmth of the family home — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/parenting/indian-lullabies-sleep-babies-learning-culture-language-benefits-parenting-10879782)

**Marathi angai**
- Recent Marathi "angai" titles are mostly modern compositions, e.g., Swanand Kirkire's "Angaai" for the Marathi film *Super Duperr* (lyrics Kunal Karan, 2026) — [IANS](https://ianslive.in/swanand-kirkire-on-singing-lullaby-for-super-duperr-there-is-an-innocence--20260407093115). **Hindi / Urdu lori: folk vs film**
- Popular Hindi lori motifs: "nindiya" (Sleep addressed as a person), "lalla ... lori" with food imagery (a bowl of milk), and comparing the child to the moon and sun — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/parenting/indian-lullabies-sleep-babies-learning-culture-language-benefits-parenting-10879782)
- A USC folklore archive entry records a Hindi "Chanda Mama" lullaby learned from a father. The informant called it "pretty nonsensical" but noted its theme of letting parents eat first out of respect — [USC Folklore Archive, 2014](https://folklore.usc.edu/?p=25121) (single informant)

**Revival and decline**
- Musicians Deepthi Bhaskar and Akash Narendran (citylamps, Bengaluru) collected lullabies across Indian languages for "Songs of Sleep" and first performed the set in 2019 — [Open magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/?p=75917)

Tamil lullaby decline is documented in The Hindu, "A requiem for Tamil lullaby" (2016), cited in the [ICOLLITE abstract](https://icollite.upi.edu/file/ppt/ABS-_ICOLLITE-25184-_The_Evolution_and_Socio-Cultural_Reflections_of_Tamil_Lullabies.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Generic folk motifs that are free to use in original lyrics** (as ideas, not wording): Uncle Moon as a friendly relative, Sleep personified as a visiting aunt or fairy ("Nindiya Rani"), the old woman spinning on the moon, the cradle or jhoola rocking, stars as the sky's lamps, birds returning to nests, the cow and calf, the maternal uncle bringing small treats, mango and jasmine imagery, and comparing the child to the moon, a lotus or a pearl.
- **Hooks to avoid because they are film songs and copyrighted:** "chanda mama door ke", "nanhi kali sone chali", "chanda hai tu", "main gaaun tum so jaao", "so jaa rajkumari", "chanda re", and any line or melody close to them. "Lalla ... lori" is widely sung. This research did not confirm whether the best-known version is folk or film (a 1970s film version is believed to exist), so avoid that hook too.
- **Public-domain-by-age works** (Omanathinkal Kidavo, 1813; Annamacharya's Jo Achyutananda, 15th century) are good for studying structure only. Modern recordings and arrangements are copyrighted. Cheeko's rule is to never use real songs anyway.
- **Devotional content:** many South Indian lullabies are devotional (Krishna, Venkateswara). A multi-faith product should default to secular nature and family imagery. Optional faith-specific packs could be considered separately.
- **Language map for refrains in original songs:** Tamil-style "aaraaro" vocables, Telugu "laali", Kannada "jo jo", Hindi "lori / ninni / so ja / nindiya", Bengali "ghum / aaye ghum", Malayalam "omane" (darling, from omana). Use them as generic vocables with fresh surrounding lyrics. Note: "omane" as a Malayalam endearment comes from general knowledge and was not separately sourced here. Check with a native speaker.
- **Gujarati values data:** moral and social values (love, security, fearlessness, togetherness) dominate traditional lullabies. These map well onto Cheeko's values-light bedtime content without being preachy.

### Gaps
- No Sahapedia page on lullabies surfaced in search. Sahapedia may still have relevant essays that a targeted site visit would find.
- No authoritative, scholarly folk-vs-film classification of famous Hindi loris was found. The "Lalla lori" origin is unverified.
- No systematic thematic study was found for Kannada jogula, Telugu jola paata or Marathi angai. Evidence is from dictionaries, a popular roundup and specific famous compositions.
- No Bhojpuri- or Awadhi-specific lullaby scholarship was found online. Related birth songs (sohar) are documented instead.
- The Better India roundup is a popular parenting article and does not cite primary sources for its regional claims.

## 5. Bilingual and Hinglish approaches by Indian kids' content creators

### Takeaway
Big Indian kids' channels publish Hindi and English rhymes side by side, usually as separate songs rather than code-mixed ones. No creator focused specifically on Hinglish (code-mixed) lullabies was found. Indian homes are often multilingual, and bedtime is one place where the home language survives, which supports a "home language refrain plus English or Hindi verse" design.

### Cited Findings
- Bilingual or dual-catalogue channels include Wow Baby Song (Hindi and English nursery rhymes and toddler songs), Kids TV India / USP Studios (English and Hindi), HooplaKidz Hindi (recurring characters Annie, Ben and Mango) and Fun For Kids TV Hindi Rhymes (3+, about 22.4M subscribers per an aggregator, unverified) — [Qoruz: HooplaKidz Hindi](https://qoruz.com/Hindi_Rhymes_For_Kids_HooplaKidz/youtube); [Qoruz: Fun For Kids TV Hindi](https://qoruz.com/@FunForKidsTVHindiRhymes/youtube); [vling channel info](https://vling.net/channel/UCyulgJOwlfldfK4TE10_LHQ/channel-info); [Roku Guide](https://www.rokuguide.com/node/19219)
- In Indian multilingual homes, a child might hear Hindi at school but fall asleep to Malayalam or Nagpuri at home. Early multi-language exposure is claimed to ease later language switching (popular claim) — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/parenting/indian-lullabies-sleep-babies-learning-culture-language-benefits-parenting-10879782)
- Carnegie Hall's Lullaby Project has produced original lullabies in more than 20 languages for over 2,800 families, with each song in the parent's own style and language — [The 74 Million](https://www.the74million.org/zero2eight/carnegie-halls-lullaby-project-helps-parents-write-love-songs-to-their-infants/); [Carnegie Hall press release 2022](https://carnegiehall.org/About/Press/Press-Releases/2022/02/22/Carnegie-Halls-Early-Childhood-Programs-Reach-Hundreds-of-Families-this-Season)
- Infants relax to lullabies even in unfamiliar languages, so melody and delivery do much of the soothing — [Harvard Gazette 2020](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2020/10/research-shows-lullabies-in-any-language-relax-babies)
- Bengali scholars call for "quality culturally rooted content" in digital spaces as a remedy for screen-driven loss of lullabies, citing Meena, Sisimpur and puppetry as precedents — [Daily Star / ANN](https://asianews.network/?p=305355)

### Inferences
- Since soothing does not depend on understanding the words, Hinglish lullabies can mix languages freely as long as the vocable refrain and melody stay constant. One workable pattern: a Hindi (or regional) vocable refrain, English verses for ages 3-8, and a Hindi or regional closing hum. Another: alternate one Hindi line with one English line in each couplet, rhyming across languages (e.g., a Hindi line ending in "-aa" answered by an English line ending in "-ah" or "-ar").
- Keep code-switching at line boundaries, not mid-phrase, so each line stays singable and the rhyme lands cleanly.
- Offer the same lullaby as English, Hindi and Hinglish versions sharing one melody, so families can choose and children recognise the tune across languages. This matches the Lullaby Project's "your own language" principle.

### Gaps
- No research or creator case study on code-mixed (Hinglish) lullabies specifically was found.
- No Indian kids' channel was found that specialises in original lullabies (as opposed to rhymes) with documented writing practices.

## 6. Guidance from songwriters and music therapists on writing lullabies

### Takeaway
Practitioner guidance agrees: keep it simple, personal and repetitive. Favour long open vowels and voiced continuants (m, n, ng, l) over hard stops. Sing slowly in a rocking meter with a simple melody and little or no accompaniment. Let the song thin out into humming. Music-therapy practice emphasises the caregiver's live voice, following the child's cues, and moving to humming when the child is overstimulated. Several specific numbers (e.g., 60-80 bpm) come from blogs and are weakly sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Songwriting Q&A advice: polish vowel sounds after drafting. Have "very few words or lines which end on hard consonants (K, T)" so the sound flows. Say lines aloud in a sing-song to test singability. Lean on stretched vowels and voiced N and NG sounds — [Writing Codidact, "Writing a Lullaby" (via search snippet; page returned 403 on fetch)](https://writing.codidact.com/q/4681)
- Soft sounds such as m, l and s are said to calm, and sharp or loud sounds should be avoided. A tempo of about 60-80 bpm with slow, steady rhythms is recommended, mirroring relaxed breathing — [Switching Styles blog](https://www.switchingstyles.ca/post/soothing-lullabies-that-will-rock-your-baby-to-sleep) (single blog, weak)
- Melodic leaps in lullabies are mostly thirds, with fourths and fifths rarer and octaves rarer still (one forum poster's analysis) — [Young Composers forum](https://youngcomposers.com/t42211/how-to-go-about-composing-a-lullaby) (anecdotal)
- Lullabies alternate tonic and dominant harmony and are almost never accompanied. Infants lose interest during sequences of dissonant intervals — [Wikipedia: Lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lullaby)
- Singer-songwriter Emily Eagen advises simple, personal lullabies that use the baby's name, and says not to over-stress what the perfect lullaby sounds like. A known tune can carry new words about the day — [Big Heart World](https://www.bigheartworld.org/?p=11882)
- Carnegie Hall Lullaby Project method: the parent first writes a letter to the child about feelings, hopes and dreams. That letter becomes the lyrics, co-written with a professional musician. Melodies are fitted to the parent's own style and language, and the family gets a recording — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/the-lullaby-project-parents-to-be-write-lullabies-for-their-unborn-babies/); [The 74 Million](https://www.the74million.org/zero2eight/carnegie-halls-lullaby-project-helps-parents-write-love-songs-to-their-infants/)
- Music therapy "song of kin" (Joanne Loewy, Mount Sinai): parents choose a culturally meaningful song, often a lullaby, which the therapist uses live, entrained to the infant's vital signs. Loewy et al. (2013, Pediatrics; 272 premature infants) found live music therapy affected vital signs, feeding and sleep. It also supported parents' bonding and expression of hope — [Mount Sinai Scholars](https://scholars.mssm.edu/en/publications/nicu-music-therapy-song-of-kin-as-critical-lullaby-in-research-an-2/); [Wikipedia: Lullaby (citing Loewy & Stewart 2013)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lullaby)
- NICU protocol: introduce sound first (live lullaby), then touch, then rocking. If the infant shows stress, hum instead of singing words — [Dräger NICU Music document](https://www.draeger.com/Content/Documents/Content/NICU_Music_Final.pdf)
- A parent's voice helps the baby feel safe and heard — [CHOC handout](https://health.choc.org/handout/music-therapy-for-the-infant-receiving-comfort-care/). Newborns prefer their mother's voice (DeCasper & Fifer, 1980) — [Wikipedia: Lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lullaby)
- Lullabies are "less happy sounding, kind of less exciting" than other song types (Manvir Singh on the Mehr lab findings) — [search summary of Science Friday / Harvard coverage](https://www.sciencefriday.com/segments/is-music-a-shared-language/)

### Inferences
Lyric-craft checklist for Cheeko writers (synthesised from the above):
1. **Sound before sense:** draft, then sing it aloud slowly. Replace words that end in t, k, p, ch or hard d where a line or held note ends. Prefer endings in aa, o, oo, ee, n, m, ng, l, re, na, ri.
2. **Long open vowels on held notes:** aa, o, oo in Hindi and Tamil-style vocables are naturally good (aaraaro, lori, so ja, nindiya, laali).
3. **Few consonant clusters:** avoid tongue-twisters, plosive alliteration (big bad bear bounces) and fast lists.
4. **Low arousal content:** no exclamations, questions that demand an answer, jokes that make children laugh hard, counting games that excite, or calls to action ("let's go!"). Humour should be a soft smile, e.g., a sleepy owl yawning.
5. **Repetition:** the refrain should recur at least three times. Verses should reuse the same sentence frame with one word swapped (parallel structure).
6. **Personal hook:** a name slot, plus optional "favourites" (toy, animal, colour) and one hope or blessing line. This copies the Lullaby Project's letter-to-child method.
7. **Fade:** the last refrain reduces to name plus vocable, then hum. No big final chord or sudden stop.
8. **Voice and arrangement** (for the audio team): one warm voice, minimal or no accompaniment, a rocking 6/8 or 3/4 feel, a slow tempo, and falling phrase endings. The ~60-80 bpm figure is a reasonable starting point but is only blog-sourced.
9. **Originality guard:** never borrow a famous lori's first line, title phrase or melody contour. Build each song from generic motifs (moon-uncle, sleep-fairy, cradle, birds home) with new wording. This follows Cheeko's standing "no real songs" rule.

### Gaps

- The 60-80 bpm tempo range has only a single blog source. No peer-reviewed tempo target for lullabies was found, though the research consistently says "slower".
- No formal Loewy or AMTA published guideline on composing lullaby lyrics was found. "Song of kin" is about choosing and performing culturally meaningful songs, not writing lyrics.
- The Carnegie Hall Lullaby Project journal PDF and the qz.com "art of writing the perfect lullaby" article returned HTTP 403, so their step-by-step prompts could not be checked directly.
