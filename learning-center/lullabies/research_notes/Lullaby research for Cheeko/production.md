# Lullaby and baby-sleep music production: a playbook for original Cheeko lullabies

Research date: 2026-10-09. Scope: production patterns and methods only. Nothing here is a melody, lyric or arrangement to copy. Cheeko already synthesises music box, harp, pads, bansuri and tanpura in `lullaby.py` (3/4 at 72 bpm, stereo, mastered to -20 LUFS, reverb RT 2.4 s, wet 0.32). Cheeko also has MiniMax TTS voices. Several vendor terms changed in Aug to Oct 2026. Those changes are flagged inline with "[CHANGED]".

## 1. What instruments do popular lullaby and baby-sleep tracks use, including Indian lullabies?

### Takeaway
The dominant commercial baby-sleep palette is soft mallets and bells: glockenspiel, vibraphone, celesta, music box, xylophone and harp, plus Mellotron or pad textures. Real Indian lullabies (lori) are mainly unaccompanied singing or humming. Bansuri, santoor and strings come from film and devotional or meditative traditions, not documented lori practice.

### Cited Findings
- Rockabye Baby, the best-known commercial baby-lullaby label, describes its sound as "a mix of glockenspiel, vibraphone, Mellotron, harps and bells" — [Rockabye Baby producer interview (Leo Flynn II)](https://rockabyebabymusic.com/blogs/rockabye-baby/interview-with-rockabye-baby-to-queen-producer-leo-flynn-ii); [CBS8](https://www.cbs8.com/article/news/rockabye-baby-turns-rock-music-into-lullabies/509-8d5e5ad0-813f-4391-b5cd-78aae2be2572)
- Producer Leo Flynn II lists xylophones, woodblocks, celestas and glockenspiels. He says the process starts by "assembling a palette of the gentlest lullaby instruments" the team can find or build, then rebuilding the song's key elements on them — [Rockabye Baby blog](https://rockabyebabymusic.com/blogs/rockabye-baby/interview-with-rockabye-baby-to-queen-producer-leo-flynn-ii)
- Rockabye Baby's early albums were mostly performed by one musician, Michael Armstrong, mainly on glockenspiel, vibraphone, Mellotron and some piano. The label ruled out grand piano, strings, flutes and acoustic guitar for brand reasons: it did not want "chamber music" or "Renaissance fair" sounds. This is one label's style choice, not a rule. Source dates from 2007 — [Austin Chronicle](https://austinchronicle.com/music/2007-08-03/510935)
- Cross-cultural research (Mehr et al., *Science* 2019) finds lullabies are rhythmically and melodically simpler and slower than other songs. They are typically sung by one female voice and are low-arousal — [Cosmos](https://cosmosmagazine.com/biology/music-really-is-a-universal-language); [Penn State](https://www.psu.edu/news/research/story/study-reveals-human-song-universal)
- Lullabies "rarely include instrumental accompaniment," and infants appear to prefer unaccompanied singing — [Wikipedia: Lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lullaby)
- Indian lori coverage centres on a mother humming and a grandmother singing while rocking a wooden cradle, so it is a voice-first tradition. Hindi film lullabies are described as slow with gentle orchestration, often strings, flute or soft percussion. This comes from a search summary of these pages, and I did not confirm which page makes the film-lullaby point — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/parenting/indian-lullabies-sleep-babies-learning-culture-language-benefits-parenting-10879782); [Open Magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/feature/sing-me-to-sleep)
- A Trehub/Trainor comparison of English and Hindi mothers found that Hindi mothers tended to sing soothing songs, while English-speaking mothers tended to sing rousing playsongs — [McMaster Trainor Lab publications](https://trainorlab.mcmaster.ca/publications/TrehubEtAl1997)
- Bansuri is a keyless bamboo transverse flute from Hindustani classical music. It is linked with Krishna and calm, and is used in classical, folk and meditative settings — [Wordnik: bansuri](https://www.wordnik.com/words/bansuri)
- Santoor is a trapezoid hammered dulcimer. It was the backbone of Kashmiri sufiana music, and its sound is described as hypnotic and tranquil — [Wikipedia: Santoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santoor); [Village Square](https://villagesquare.in/fading-notes-of-kashmiri-santoor/)
- A commercial stock track titled "Indian Lullaby" uses bansuri melody with dilruba and Indian percussion. It shows the stock-music convention, not traditional practice — [Shockwave-Sound](https://shockwave-sound.com/stock-music-track/18593/indian-lullaby)

### Inferences
- Cheeko's current palette (music box, harp, pad, bansuri, tanpura drone, swarmandal shimmer) already matches the mainstream baby-sleep palette. Celesta, glockenspiel or vibraphone and a soft santoor-like hammered tremolo would extend it without new risk.
- For an Indian identity, the strongest authentic signal is the voice: a hummed or sung lori-style line. Instrument choice matters less. A version with a "voice plus drone only" arrangement would match both the lori tradition and the research that infants prefer unaccompanied singing.
- Avoid percussion beyond a very soft rocking pulse. Sources describe lullaby beats as gentle or less steady (see section 7).

### Gaps
- I found no ethnomusicological source documenting bansuri, santoor, sitar or veena as traditional lori accompaniment. Their use in Indian "sleep music" appears to be a modern stock or meditation convention.
- No production breakdowns were found for ChuChu TV or Infobells lullabies (instrument lists or arrangements).

## 2. Vocal style: how should lullabies be sung, and how do kids' channels record them?

### Takeaway
Research supports a single, warm, unhurried female-type voice with raised pitch, slower tempo, long pauses between phrases, and a smooth legato with few accents. Hummed and wordless versions are an accepted format. I found no public interviews describing how Super Simple Songs, Cocomelon, ChuChu TV or Infobells record or mix their lullaby vocals.

### Cited Findings
- Infant-directed singing by parents had higher pitch and slower tempo than the same song sung without a baby. Raters judged it more emotionally engaging, and the pitch and tempo differences predicted those ratings (Trehub et al. 1997) — [Trainor Lab](https://trainorlab.mcmaster.ca/publications/TrehubEtAl1997); [Trehub 1993 PDF](https://trainorlab.mcmaster.ca/publications/pdfs/1993_Trehub.pdf)
- Acoustic analysis of infant-directed lullabies found slower tempo, relatively more low-frequency energy, longer pauses between phrases, and higher pitch and jitter than non-infant versions — [Trainor et al. 1997 PDF](https://trainorlab.mcmaster.ca/publications/pdfs/trainor_et_al_1997.pdf)
- Infants preferred higher-pitched recordings when given a choice (Trainor & Zacharias 1998) — [Trainor & Zacharias 1998 PDF](https://trainorlab.mcmaster.ca/publications/pdfs/trainor_zacharias_1998.pdf)
- Infant-directed singing across cultures is characterised by "greater emotional voice quality, raised pitch level, and slower rate or tempo" — [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/your-musical-self/201107/does-singing-to-your-baby-really-work)
- Laurel Trainor contrasts accented, rhythmic "rousing" singing with smooth, legato singing that does not emphasise accents. Lullabies are the second kind — [NPR (2023)](https://www.npr.org/2023/05/26/1178496342/what-is-it-about-a-lullaby-that-helps-kids-fall-asleep)
- A podcast on infant sleep stresses smooth, connected phrasing, warm tone and a narrow pitch range. It also says how a lullaby is sung matters as much as its tempo. This is practitioner opinion — [M. Peterson Music, Ep. 137 transcript](https://mpetersonmusic.com/ep-137-transcript)
- Spotify's lullaby project with producer Pontus Winnberg and a sleep expert recorded four versions of each new lullaby: English, Swedish, instrumental and hummed — [Spotify Newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2021-05-07/spotify-for-the-record-explores-the-art-and-science-of-lullabies/)
- A sleep-music retail guide argues that lyrics engage language processing, while wordless "ahs" or humming are usually fine for sleep. This is a commercial source and lower confidence — [Mattress Miracle](https://mattressmiracle.ca/blogs/mattress-miracle-blog/best-soft-music)
- Super Simple Songs has run its own Toronto studio since 2015, with an in-house team of over 40 composers, musicians, animators, puppeteers and educators. No lullaby-specific vocal method is described — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/super-simple-songs-20th-anniversary-youtube/); [Super Simple studio visit](https://supersimple.com/caities-classroom/lets-visit-a-music-studio/)

### Inferences
- Use a calm female voice (Cheeko calm, `English_CalmWoman`, `English_Graceful_Lady`) and slow the delivery. Leave long gaps between lines, as the existing "goodnight" sample C already does. Avoid upbeat or "playful" voice IDs for sleep content.
- Produce each lullaby in up to three variants: sung or spoken with words, hummed or "ooh", and instrumental. This follows the Spotify project and gives parents a choice for very young babies versus 4-to-8-year-olds who like words.
- Close-mic, breathy intimacy is a common aesthetic, but no source here established it. Treat it as a style choice. With TTS, the practical equivalent is a soft voice, little reverb on the voice, and no harsh sibilance (see section 3).
- Male lullaby voices are acceptable. Research describes the typical lullaby singer as one female, but fathers' infant-directed singing showed the same pitch and tempo shifts (Trehub 1997).

### Gaps
- No primary sources were found on how Super Simple Songs, Cocomelon, ChuChu TV or Infobells record, comp, tune or mix lullaby vocals (mic choice, compression or reverb settings).
- No study isolates breathiness as a soothing factor. Jitter and "emotional voice quality" are the nearest measured features.

## 3. Mixing and mastering sleep audio: loudness, peaks, compression, EQ, stereo, reverb, fades and noise beds

### Takeaway
No authoritative standard sets a LUFS target specifically for lullabies. The defensible approach is to master calm content at the quiet end of published music-streaming guidance (about -16 to -20 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ -1 dBTP) and keep short-term loudness flat. Long fades and a hard playback-volume ceiling matter more than the master level, because infant-sleep-machine research shows the real risk is SPL at the crib.

### Cited Findings
**Loudness standards**
- Spotify normalises to -14 LUFS by default, with -19 "Quiet" and -11 "Loud" options. Quieter masters are turned up only as far as true-peak headroom allows: a -20 LUFS track peaking at -5 dBTP is lifted only to -16 LUFS. Spotify recommends true peak below -1 dBTP, or below -2 dBTP if louder than -14 LUFS — [Spotify for Artists: Loudness normalization](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/loudness-normalization/)
- EBU R128 sets broadcast loudness at -23 LUFS ±0.5 LU. Its streaming supplement (R128 s2) allows -18 LUFS, and -16 LUFS for music-heavy content with a peak-to-loudness ratio of 15 dB or less — [Wikipedia: EBU R 128](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EBU_R_128); [EBU R128 s2 PDF](https://knowledgehub.ebu.ch/files/live/sites/tech/files/shared/r/r128s2v1_0.pdf)
- AES TD1004 (2015) recommended -16 to -20 LUFS with a maximum true peak of -1 dBTP for streaming. It was superseded by TD1008, now standard AES77, which recommends a maximum true peak of -1 dBTP at the codec input — [Production Expert on AES](https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/aes-update-loudness-recommendations-for-audio-only-streaming); [Grimm Audio on AES TD1008](https://www.grimmaudio.com/?p=4698)
- Apple Music normalises to -16 LUFS with Sound Check, and YouTube to about -14 LUFS. These figures come from third-party sources. Sources conflict on whether YouTube and Apple turn quiet tracks up or only turn loud tracks down — [Production Expert (Apple -16)](https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/apple-choose-16lufs-loudness-level-for-apple-music-heres-why?format=amp); [Production Advice (YouTube)](https://productionadvice.co.uk/youtube-music/); [Wikipedia R128 (downward-only claim)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EBU_R_128)
- General mastering guidance puts typical masters at -12 to -16 LUFS with true peak between -1 and -2 dB. That band is aimed at regular music, not sleep — [Mastering The Mix](https://masteringthemix.com/blogs/learn/mixing-and-mastering-using-lufs)
- Sleep-music curators recommend restrained dynamics (a small loudness range) so that "sudden peaks" do not jolt listeners awake. This is low confidence because the site's ratings are AI-generated — [Mattress Miracle](https://mattressmiracle.ca/blogs/mattress-miracle-blog/best-soft-music); [MusicIWant: low-intensity music for sleep](https://musiciwant.com/guide/low-intensity-music-for-sleep)

**Safe playback level (most relevant for a device)**
- Hugh et al., *Pediatrics* 2014 (133:677), measured 14 infant sleep machines at maximum volume. They averaged 79 dBA at 30 cm (crib rail), 71 dBA at 100 cm and 63 dBA at 200 cm. Three exceeded 85 dBA at 30 cm, the adult 8-hour workplace limit, and the loudest reached 92.9 dBA. All but one exceeded 50 dBA at every distance — [NEJM Journal Watch](https://clinician.nejm.org/sleep-machines-damage-infant-hearing-nejm-jw.NA34176); [Safety+Health](https://safetyandhealthmagazine.com/articles/10032-white-noise-from-sleep-machines-can-harm-babies-ears-study-finds)
- The recommended limit for hospital nurseries and NICUs is 50 dBA averaged over one hour. The authors advised placing machines far from the infant (never in or on the crib), using the lowest volume, and running them only until the baby is asleep — [ENTtoday](https://enttoday.org/?p=9218); [AHC Health eNews](https://www.ahchealthenews.com/2014/03/04/how-sleep-machines-can-harm-your-babys-hearing/)

**Noise beds, heartbeat and womb sounds**
- Spencer et al. 1990 randomised 40 neonates aged 2 to 7 days. 80% (16 of 20) fell asleep within 5 minutes with white noise, versus 25% (5 of 20) of controls. It measured only sleep onset — [Archives of Disease in Childhood](https://adc.bmj.com/content/65/1/135); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1792397/)
- A pooled analysis of 38 studies across ages (Riedy 2021) rated the evidence for continuous noise and sleep as "very low" quality — [Slumber Studios summary](https://slumberstudios.com/blog/white-noise-for-babies/)
- A 2021 trial in 103 stable NICU infants found no effect of mother's voice or white noise on sleep quality, cortisol or heart rate. A retrospective preterm study linked maternal heartbeat plus white noise to lower heart rate and better sleep, but cannot show cause and effect — [Annals of Palliative Medicine / AME](https://apm.amegroups.org/article/view/107189/html); [Cradlewise summary](https://cradlewise.com/blog/can-white-noise-help-my-baby-sleep)
- A sleep consultant cautions that rhythmic sounds like heartbeats may become stimulating for some children. This is opinion, not a finding — [Cradlewise](https://cradlewise.com/blog/can-white-noise-help-my-baby-sleep)
- In the womb, low frequencies around 125 Hz arrive slightly amplified and highs are damped by up to 10 dB (Richards 1992, via secondary source) — [Lenabo summary](https://lenabo.com/en/blogs/news/white-noise-for-babies-effects-research-and-safe-use)

### Inferences
- **House loudness for Cheeko:** Cheeko controls its own playback and content is not on Spotify, so consistency across Cheeko's own library matters most. A reasonable house rule: regular kids' songs at about -16 LUFS, sleep content at about -20 to -23 LUFS integrated, all with true peak ≤ -1 dBTP. Sleep content would then play 4 to 7 dB quieter than playtime songs at the same knob position. `lullaby.py` already masters to -20 LUFS, which sits at the bottom of AES TD1004's -16 to -20 window.
- **Short-term flatness:** Keep the short-term LUFS (3 s window) within a few LU of the integrated value. Use slow, gentle compression (low ratio, long attack and release) or volume automation rather than fast limiting. Avoid any transient louder than the melody, such as a music-box pin click or harp pluck attack. The existing `musicbox()` adds a click noise burst, so keep it quiet.
- **EQ:** Roll off harsh highs with a gentle low-pass or high shelf, perhaps from about 6 to 8 kHz. Tame sibilance on TTS. The womb is high-damped, and the reverb in `lullaby.py` already low-passes its impulse response at 4.2 kHz. Do not add sub-bass; it will not reproduce on the toy speaker (section 5).
- **Fades:** Use long fade-ins (several seconds) so the track never starts abruptly, and a long fade-out at the end. The script defaults to a 6 s fade. Consider 20 to 60 s for long-form sleep tracks and a gradual tempo slowdown into the end, which sample A already does.
- **Device volume:** The strongest safety lever is in firmware, not the mix. Cap maximum volume in sleep mode, add an auto-off timer and a slow fade-to-silence. Target well under 50 dBA at the child's position. Measure the real SPL of the Cheeko speaker at 30 cm and 1 m with a sound level meter.
- **Noise beds:** Pink or brown noise under a lullaby is optional. Evidence of benefit is weak and limited to onset, and a heartbeat may stimulate. If offered, make it a separate, quieter option and do not bake it into songs.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or industry source gives a lullaby-specific LUFS or loudness-range target. The -20 to -23 LUFS house target above is an inference.
- No primary source on reverb amounts (wet percentage or RT60) for lullabies was found.
- AAP-specific distance guidance (for example the "7 feet" figure on consumer blogs) was not verified.

## 4. How long-form sleep tracks (1 to 10 hours) are built

### Takeaway
Long sleep videos are made by building one seamless, loop-friendly segment (minutes to about 30 minutes), crossfading its seams, rendering it once, and repeating that file to length. Drones, pads and nature beds loop well; beat-driven material does not, and very short loops sound repetitive.

### Cited Findings
- Ambient, drone, pad and nature material is far easier to extend than beat-based music. Sleep videos often rest on one sustained theme or a rain or wave bed run for hours — [TunePocket: long meditation music](https://www.tunepocket.com/how-to-make-long-meditation-music/); [TunePocket: long tracks from short clips](https://www.tunepocket.com/make-long-music-tracks/)
- Trim the start and end before looping so the crossfade is seamless. In Premiere, select all repeated clips and press Shift+D to crossfade every join — [Adobe Community (Premiere)](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-discussions/how-to-create-a-seamless-audio-loop-via-premiere-pro/m-p/13463242)
- Loops of 10 to 20 seconds are "far too short for ambience" because the repetition becomes obvious after a few cycles. This is one forum respondent's opinion from 2020 — [Adobe Community (After Effects)](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/need-to-make-a-looping-animation-for-a-10-hour-video/m-p/11620714)
- Arithmetic example: a 30-minute loop repeated 20 times makes 10 hours. Very long editor timelines get sluggish, so render the loop first and then concatenate — [TunePocket](https://www.tunepocket.com/make-long-music-tracks/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended Cheeko long-form recipe:**
  1. Compose a 3-to-5-minute piece whose last bar resolves back to the opening chord or drone.
  2. Render a version with no intro and no outro.
  3. Make the seam with an equal-power crossfade of 2 to 8 s inside a sustained pad or drone, not on a melodic attack.
  4. On the device, play intro, then the loop N times, then an outro with a long fade.
  5. Over the first 10 to 20 minutes, gradually thin the arrangement (melody first, then harmony, leaving drone or noise) and lower the level by a few dB. This "dimming" approach is inferred; I found no published standard.
- On the device, prefer looping in firmware or the player (with a crossfade) over storing 1-to-10-hour files. This saves storage, and the player can randomise variations to reduce repetition.
- Code-synthesised music has an advantage here. Each loop pass can be regenerated with small seeded variations (different arpeggio inversions, ornament timing, or a bansuri phrase order), avoiding exact repetition.

### Gaps
- No interviews with producers of 1-to-10-hour baby-sleep channels were found describing their slowing or dimming practice. That pattern is inferred.
- No research was found on whether infants or children sleep better with varied versus exactly repeated loops.

## 5. Small toy-sized speakers: what carries, bass and mono

### Takeaway
Small speakers reproduce roughly 500 Hz to 6–10 kHz well and lose most fundamentals below 300–600 Hz. Bass must be implied through harmonics in the 400 Hz to 6 kHz range. The low end should be mono, and mixes should be checked summed to mono, through a 400 Hz–6 kHz band-pass and at low volume.

### Cited Findings
- Phone speakers lose most content below about 500 Hz and above 6 kHz — [SoundGirls: Lost in Translation](https://soundgirls.org/lost-in-translation/)
- A measured iPhone 7 Plus speaker falls off sharply below 600 Hz and above 9 kHz, leaving about 500 Hz to 10 kHz usable — [Sonarworks](https://www.sonarworks.com/blog/learn/make-your-mix-sound-great-on-small-speakers)
- Instead of boosting below 80 Hz, EQ higher to bring out the bass's harmonics. Doubling a bass line with a higher-harmonic layer keeps it audible on small speakers — [Sonarworks](https://www.sonarworks.com/blog/learn/make-your-mix-sound-great-on-small-speakers)
- Saturation adds harmonics that "ripple up the frequency spectrum into the 400 Hz – 6K range," letting listeners infer bass on small speakers. Monitoring at low volume exposes small-speaker problems — [Waves: 8 tips for small speakers](https://www.waves.com/tips-for-great-mixes-on-small-speakers)
- High-pass gently below the lowest useful note to remove rumble that "eats headroom." Keep low frequencies centred, because wide bass can vanish in mono — [Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-mix-bass/)
- To simulate small systems, monitor through a 400 Hz–6 kHz band-pass and check the mix in mono — [Sonarworks](https://www.sonarworks.com/blog/learn/make-your-mix-sound-great-on-small-speakers)
- Psychoacoustic bass plugins (for example CodWaves BassXtend) synthesise harmonics above a chosen root frequency. These are vendor claims — [Production Expert](https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/codwaves-bassxtend-can-it-rescue-bass-on-small-speakers)

### Inferences
- `soft_bass()` in `lullaby.py` is a pure sine, so on a toy speaker it will mostly be inaudible. It only eats amplifier headroom and may cause distortion or "chuffing" at higher volumes. For on-device masters:
  - high-pass at roughly 150 to 250 Hz (tune to the measured speaker);
  - give the bass and drone voices 2nd and 3rd harmonics (gentle saturation or added partials);
  - let the tanpura and harp carry the low-mid warmth around 200 to 500 Hz.
- Cheeko almost certainly has one mono speaker. Masters should be mono-compatible, or delivered as mono. Equal-power panning and decorrelated stereo reverb (as in `lullaby.py`) can lose or comb-filter when summed. Render a mono device master and check that the reverb level still sounds right after summing.
- Keep the melody (music box, bansuri, voice) in about 500 Hz to 3 kHz, where small speakers are most efficient. Use the 6–10 kHz shimmer sparingly: it carries, but it is also where "harshness" lives in a dark room.
- Process sleep and playtime masters separately for the device (band-limited, mono, quieter) and for marketing videos or phones (full-range stereo).

### Gaps
- The Cheeko speaker's actual frequency response, maximum SPL and distortion curve are unknown. Measure them with a test sweep and an SPL meter to set the exact high-pass and EQ.
- Whether the firmware applies its own EQ, DRC or limiter (which would interact with the master) was not checked.

## 7. Published tips from music therapists, researchers and sleep-music producers

### Takeaway
Research and music-therapy practice converge on a few principles for lullabies: slow, simple and repetitive, small pitch range, smooth with little accent, a soft steady volume, and one warm voice. Familiar, personally meaningful songs (the "song of kin") and live adaptation to the child work best. Infants relax even to unfamiliar lullabies, which supports making new originals.

### Cited Findings
- Bainbridge et al. (*Nature Human Behaviour*, 2020/2021) played unfamiliar foreign lullabies to 144 US infants aged 2 to 14 months. Heart rate and pupil size fell and electrodermal activity was dampened, compared with non-lullaby songs from the same cultures. The effect was unrelated to age. Clips were only 14 s each — [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2020/10/research-shows-lullabies-in-any-language-relax-babies); [CogSci paper](https://cognitivesciencesociety.org/cogsci20/papers/0591/index.html); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nathum/v5y2021i2d10.1038_s41562-020-00963-z.html)
- In that study's song set, lullabies tended to be slower, with smaller pitch ranges and a less steady beat — [BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/babies-relax-when-listening-unfamiliar-lullabies-other-cultures)
- Mehr et al. (*Science* 2019, 366:6468): accent, tempo and pitch range predict a song's social function (lullaby versus dance, healing or love) across cultures — [Penn State](https://www.psu.edu/news/research/story/study-reveals-human-song-universal); [Princeton](https://molbio.princeton.edu/publications/universality-and-diversity-human-song)
- Durham University's Annaliese Grimaud (press coverage) lists typical lullaby traits: a gently paced beat, soft volume, simple rhythms that may mimic rocking, simple repeated melodies and higher pitch — [Irish Post](https://www.irishpost.com/news/u2-song-named-among-top-10-tracks-to-send-kids-off-sleep-171249)
- Tempo figures conflict:
  - a TU Dresden conference poster reports about 60 to 80 BPM linked to lower infant heart rate and stress markers, and lists tempo consistency across cultures as an open question;
  - a 2019 brand analysis of Spotify lullabies found an average of about 91 BPM, in C major and 4/4. This is a commercial, non-peer-reviewed source.
  - [TU Dresden poster PDF](https://tu-dresden.de/ing/elektrotechnik/ias/socialtouch/ressourcen/dateien/Poster-IASAT25-Benjamin-2.pdf); [Irish Post](https://www.irishpost.com/news/u2-song-named-among-top-10-tracks-to-send-kids-off-sleep-171249)
- Loewy et al. (*Pediatrics* 2013, 131(5):902–918) was a randomised multisite trial with 272 premature infants in 11 NICUs. Live parent-preferred lullabies, an "ocean disc" (rolling-bead whoosh) and a gato box matched to heartbeat improved heart rate, sucking, sleep patterns and calorie intake, and eased parental stress. The authors caution that recorded music can overstimulate newborns, whereas live music adapts to the infant — [LiveScience](https://livescience.com/28710-music-therapy-premature-babies-health.html); [HealthDay](https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/pregnancy/music-aids-preemie-physiologic-developmental-function-675337.html); [Drexel Q&A](https://drexel.edu/news/archive/2013/May/QandA-Paul-Nolan)
- "Song of kin" is a music-therapy method in which parents choose a meaningful, culturally relevant song, often a lullaby, which therapists then use with the infant — [Mount Sinai Scholars](https://scholars.mssm.edu/en/publications/nicu-music-therapy-song-of-kin-as-critical-lullaby-in-research-an-2/); [Mount Sinai donor story](https://donorstories.mountsinai.org/nicu-music-therapy/)
- Trehub suggests infant-directed music helps optimise an infant's mood and regulate arousal — [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/your-musical-self/201107/does-singing-to-your-baby-really-work)
- Rockabye Baby producers aim to keep some of the original emotional character so that adults also connect with the songs. That means the parent listening matters too — [Rockabye Baby blog](https://rockabyebabymusic.com/blogs/rockabye-baby/interview-with-rockabye-baby-to-queen-producer-leo-flynn-ii)

### Inferences
- **Composition checklist for originals:**
  - slow tempo (about 60 to 80 BPM, or a 3/4 rocking feel; `lullaby.py` uses 72);
  - narrow melodic range (about an octave or less), stepwise motion and repeated short phrases;
  - falling phrase endings, few accents and a soft, gently swaying pulse rather than a hard beat;
  - steady volume and long rests between phrases;
  - one warm voice, or a hummed line.
- **Personalisation fits the "song of kin" idea:**
  - include the child's name in lyrics (TTS can render this per child);
  - offer parent-chosen favourites from Cheeko's own catalogue;
  - let parents record their own hum or voice over Cheeko's instrumental beds.
- **Adaptivity:** Live music works partly because it adapts to the child. Cheeko could approximate that by continuing to slow and soften as the session goes on, or by stopping on a timer, instead of playing a fixed loudness for hours.
- **Originality:** Infants relax to unfamiliar lullabies, so new Cheeko originals should not be less effective than traditional ones. Familiarity can then be built through nightly repetition of the same Cheeko lullaby.
- **Age range:** For older children (4 to 8), lyrics and gentle stories are acceptable. For 0 to 2, emphasise hummed or instrumental versions and minimal words.

### Gaps
- No controlled study ties a specific BPM to infant sleep outcomes. Published tempo ranges conflict.
- No published interviews were found with producers of large baby-sleep YouTube channels or Indian kids' channels about their methods.
- The research covers infants (0 to 14 months, plus NICU preterms). Evidence for 3-to-8-year-olds' bedtime music is not covered here.
- The Loewy 2013 full text (exact session protocol, which intervention drove which outcome) was not accessed. Secondary accounts conflict on frequency and attribution.
